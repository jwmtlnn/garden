Unboxing the RDI from Innovation Systems

  • Janne W. Matilainen (janne.matilainen@lapinamk.fi)
    • University of Lapland - Faculty of Social Sciences
    • Lapland University of Applied Sciences - Smart Built Environment
  • Research field: Social Sciences - Management/Organization Studies
  • Keywords (YSO): actor-network theory, innovation system, innovation policy, regions, research and development operations, university of applied science

1. Background

Traditionally innovation systems are treated as a stable, pre-existing macro-structures. This project investigates national innovation systems (NIS) as ongoing sociomaterial achievements that are continuously performed to exist. In the context of Finnish Universities of Applied Sciences' (UAS), this performance occurs under regional pressures, where the university's purpose is to act as a node translating national and European innovation mandates into commercially viable research, development and innovation (RDI).

When an UAS prepares a funding proposal, it does not interact with a clean formal system. Instead, it engages with a dense, messy webs of actors and sites that are both local and global, lice digital spaces. From an Actor-Network Theory (ANT) perspective with a praxiographic approach, these digital spaces are not neutral tools or mediators. They are actors and the sites of practice where the innovation system is enacted into being. Because these digital interactions are fragmented across teams, institutions, and platforms, the innovation system inherently also becomes multiple. It might exists as a "budget version" in spreadsheet, a "scientific version" in the application form, a "economic version" in talks with the businesses and a "bureaucratic version" on the submission portal.

The core challenge of this research is to understand how these multiple, localized versions are coordinated and what sociomaterial acts allow this disparate network of digital traces to eventually stabilize, cross the threshold and lock together into an independent, institutionalized RDI project entity as its own "creature".

2. Research Questions

To investigate this socio-material enactment, the study is guided by one overarching question and four targeted sub-questions:

  • Q1: How is the National Innovation System socio-materially enacted by University of Applied Science through a publicly-funded RDI project?
    • Q1.1: What is the actor-network of a project preparation?
    • Q1.2: How is the innovation system enacted differently across different sites of practice?
    • Q1.3: How is the multiplicity of the innovation system coordinated?
    • Q1.4 What are the acts leading to the creation of a project as it's own entity?

3. Implementation

3.1 Data

The case is a Horizon Europe project preparation leading to a submission, called RESILAKE. It has 12 partners across Europe, Lapland UAS being the coordinating partner. Main body of data consists of meeting transcriptions, emails, spreadsheets, documents etc. concerning the project preparation in digital environments, mainly from Microsoft Teams. These are supplemented with participatory and non-participatory observations, interviews and surveys. Informed consent is gathered from the participants, and their right and ways to modify or withdraw that consent is informed and ensured. Data considering any participants/subjects is pseudo-/anonymized and handled according to best practices on the type of data in question.

3.2 Methods

A praxiographic rich description of the case is constructed by qualitative ethnographic methods, utilizing an actor-network theory -type of approach to "follow the actors". The main methodological tools for this are tracing the actor-network with its actors, translations and punctualizations. Preliminary tests are promising to identify actor-networks computationally by LightRAG, which would be a novel method to exploit LLM's in research.

3.3 Analysis Pipeline

  • Phase 1. Securing Digital Access & Ethical: Establish boundaries and secure individual and institutional consent to archive digital traces from the project preparation. This includes exporting raw communication data from Microsoft Teams, emails and version histories of collaborative documents.
  • Phase 2. Tracing and Mapping the Actor-Network (addressing Q1.1): Deploy the ANT principle of generalized symmetry and ontological agnosticism. Treat human and non-human actors as equal nodes. Map the translation processes, identifying the Obligatory Passage Points (OPPs) that actors must pass through.
  • Phase 3. Identifying Sites of Practice & Multiplicity (addressing Q1.2 & Q1.3): Perform a praxiographic analysis by flattening the data into distinct sites of practice. Compare how the project is performed across different digital zones. Document how these versions differ and track the coordination mechanisms used to prevent the network from fracturing.
  • Phase 4. Analyzing the Final Black-Boxing Trajectory (addressing Q1.4): Track the timeline up to the final submission deadline. Code the specific sociomaterial acts that collapse the fluid, messy network into a singular, durable digital "creature" able to be recognized by the funder.

3.4 Hypertext

A body of texts are produced in Markdown format in Obsidian environment. These texts form a non-linear repository of texts where the contents relate to each other, resembling a "pattern language". This emphasizes and supports the non-linearity of research both as a practice in this project and as a subject of the study. The monograph is mostly compiled out of these texts. This also promotes open science and practical impact (see x.x. Open Science).

3.5 Schedule

The project is to be done mostly as a part-time research, working full-time with the Lapland UAS. Research grant periods for full-time research are planned for analysis- and finalizing. See attached link to the Gantt-chart.

3.6 Risk Assessment

Main risk for implementation is the possible delay of the project due to part-time research. The research is thus designed to have as little timing-sensitive parts as possible, to enable flexible and resilient implementation. No ethical issues or ethical reviews foreseen and all handling of personal data is based on informed consent. Self- and faculty monitoring practiced.

4. Results Pursued

4.1 Deliverables

The primary deliverable of this research is a comprehensive, single-authored monograph. Instead of a thematic analysis, the monograph will be written as a thick empirical narrative that captures the messiness, friction and shifting alliances of the project preparation. The monograph addresses the sociomaterial translations, actor-networks, punctualization and multiplicity and its coordination of the case, including their respective schemas. Side products leading to the monograph are the aforementioned collection of hypertexts, one open-access wide audience paper and one peer-reviewed book chapter.

If actionable insights for practice arise during the study, an executive short report will be composed and delivered to regional public-private RDI managers, Lapland UAS leadership and The Rectors’ Conference of Finnish Universities of Applied Sciences.

4.2 Impact

Theoretical impact pursued is to present innovation systems not as a single, solid background structures but a multiple phenomenon that is constantly being pieced together. This work challenges the macro-bias and determinism in innovation studies. It aims to renew the discussion about technically oriented RDI work in these types of institutions and their stakeholders, with not technological or social explanations, but seeing them as the same sociomaterial continuum.

Methodological impact of the study is about using hypertext as a non-linear medium, where a linear representation of a monograph can be compiled. Computational methods for identifying and tracing actor-networks out of natural language accounts are expected to be novel experiments.

For practice, policy and business, the opening of the "black box" aims to show the transaction costs of innovation policy transmission, which might also be barriers of entry for small and medium enterprises they aim to support.

According to bibliometric searches there is little research about these types of organizations and activities in frameworks of ANT, but the project is not novel as an ANT or STS (Science, Technology & Society) study and no special potential for scientific breakthroughs is expected.

4.3 Open Science

The project promotes open science and open source by publishing the research as a rolling release "in the making" as a "Digital Garden" -type of website. This also emphasizes the non-linear and non-deterministic nature of research and innovation. An open-access wide-audience publication (publ. type D) has been published (Matilainen, 2025) to promote these approaches and frameworks in the UAS' RDI contexts.
