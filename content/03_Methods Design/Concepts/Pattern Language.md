The concept of _pattern language_ was popularized by architect Christopher Alexander and his colleagues in their book, "A Pattern Language: Towns, Buildings, Construction" [[@alexanderPatternLanguageTowns1977]]. While originating in architecture, the concept of pattern languages has been applied to other fields, including software engineering, where "design patterns" are used to document and share best practices. The concept isn't applied or practiced in this project, but its philosophy is adjacent to the [[Digital Garden]] you're currently reading at. See also the [Wikipedia article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pattern_language)

Essentially, a pattern language is:

- **A System of Solutions:**
  - It's an organized set of "patterns," where each pattern describes a recurring problem and offers a core solution that can be adapted to various contexts.
- **Design Guidance:**
  - It provides a framework for designing and building environments, from entire towns to individual rooms, in a way that promotes human well-being and a sense of place.
- **Interconnected Patterns:**
  - The patterns are interconnected, forming a "language" that allows designers and builders to create cohesive and harmonious environments.
- **Focus on Human Experience:**
  - The patterns are rooted in observations of how people interact with their surroundings and aim to create spaces that support positive human experiences.

Key aspects of a pattern language include:

- **Patterns as Problem-Solution Pairs:**
  - Each pattern consists of a description of a common problem, followed by a proposed solution.
- **Contextual Solutions:**
  - The solutions are not rigid formulas but rather guidelines that can be adapted to specific contexts and needs.
- **Hierarchical Structure:**
  - The patterns are organized in a hierarchical structure, ranging from large-scale patterns (e.g., urban planning) to small-scale patterns (e.g., room details).
