## Introduction & general definition

Controversies can be used as a tool to see and understand how the [[Sociomateriality|sociomaterial]] world is formed and transformed. They are not merely disagreements to be resolved, but rather dynamic situations where [[Actor|actors]] disagree and where established realities are put into question. A controversy refers to any aspect of science and technology that is not yet stabilized, closed, or "[[Punctualisation|black boxed]]", representing shared uncertainty. [[@venturiniDivingMagmaHow2010]][[@bignettiActornetworkTheoryMethodological2023]].

## Established definitions

Controversies are situations where actors discover they cannot ignore each other, leading to a negotiation. They are not necessarily "formal" negotiations and discussions between human actors, but point to any type of conflicts that decide, and are decided by, the distribution of power. They tend to be complex and reduction-resistant phenomena, meaning they cannot be reduced to a single question or simple answers, as actors often disagree even on the very questions to be asked. [[@latourScienceActionHow1987]],Part A.)[[@venturiniDivingMagmaHow2010]]

## Role in ANT

In ANT methodologies, [[Translation|translations]] & controversies are considered one of the best available occasions to observe the social world and its making, revealing how collective existence is constructed through collective work [[@venturiniDivingMagmaHow2010]]. They explicitly advocate for studying science and technology "in action" and following controversies under the lids of "black boxes", because these disputes provide the analyst with an essential resource to render social connections traceable [[@latourReassemblingSocialIntroduction2005]],Part I).

Rather than imposing predefined categories, ANT allows actors to deploy the full range of controversies in which they are immersed, enabling a better grasp of how the social is generated. Controversies are critical sites where "ontological politics" unfolds, highlighting how realities are enacted, shaped, and contested, and how things could always be "otherwise". [[@latourReassemblingSocialIntroduction2005]],Part I). They allow ANT to question conventional politics by foregrounding the denial of singularity and the championing of multiplicity surrounding existence [[@alcadipaniActorNetworkTheoryOrganizations2010]]p.424-425). The focus shifts from "matters of fact" to "matters of concern" in controversies, viewing disputed issues as interesting "gatherings" rather than stable objects [[@latourReassemblingSocialIntroduction2005]]p.114).

## Critical engagement

[[@venturiniDivingMagmaHow2010]] and [[@walshamActorNetworkTheoryResearch1997]], after [[@latourActornetworkTheoryFew1996]] describe studying controversies with ANT as "thorny and intricate" and making investigation "slower and harder," often producing a "mass of detail" that is difficult to manage and represent. Representing the dynamic, "magmatic movement" of controversies, where actors and networks constantly liquefy and solidify, is challenging. Some have also pointed out the difficulty of authentically representing the "voices of nonhumans" in ANT accounts, as they are always mediated through human categories, or reducing people to the status of things [[@jacksonAnalyticalMethodologicalUnderstanding2015]].

ANT has faced criticism for appearing to lack substantive political critique or having an "amoral posture," even being accused of "conniving with those in power". However, proponents argue that ANT does bring politics back in, by focusing on how realities are enacted and contested, and on the consequences of inclusions and exclusions. [[@alcadipaniActorNetworkTheoryOrganizations2010]].
