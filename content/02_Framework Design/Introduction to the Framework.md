The framework combines the bodies of thought of [[Actor-Network Theory]] (ANT) and the [[National Innovation Systems]] (NIS). It's aim is to use the ANT's sensibility of the messy [[Sociomateriality|sociomaterial]] processes and innovation in the systemic context of NIS. The NIS is taken here as a "macro/meso-level" framework that views innovation as an outcome of a complex set of relationships between institutions. It helps explain e.g. the policies, funding mechanisms, and institutional roles that shape why and how [[Universities of Applied Sciences]] (UAS) are expected to engage in research, development and innovation activities ([[RDI]]). Although the ANT denies Euclidean "levels", it's purpose here is best explained as a "micro-level" tool, stance and sensibility. The study utilizes ANT to gain insight into these systemic questions and to explain the presented systems in a process-oriented "enacting" manner.
