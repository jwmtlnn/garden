The "model of interessement" or translation model is based on the process of [[Translation]] as [[@akrichKeySuccessInnovation2002]] it describes, tries to provide a richer understanding of how innovations travel and transform the [[Sociomateriality|sociomaterial]] worlds. In ANT it is contrasted with the [[Model of Diffusion]]. It does this by focusing on the dynamic processes of network construction, the active roles of diverse actors, and the ongoing [[Controversies|negotiations]] and transformations that shape their trajectories. It moves beyond a simple linear model of spread to examine the complex web of relationships and actions that constitute innovation in practice. [[@akrichKeySuccessInnovation2002a]];[[@akrichKeySuccessInnovation2002]].

The model of interessement is based upon the principles of [[Agnosticism]], [[Generalized Symmetry]] and relies on the concept of [[Translation]] [[@akrichKeySuccessInnovation2002a]];[[@callonElementsSociologyTranslation1986]]. It suggests that innovation is a process, not a fixed result, highlighting the precarious and uncertain nature of network stabilization. The most significant difference is that the interessement/translation model focuses on the continuous, costly, and precarious work required to make things hold together and move, whereas the diffusion model assumes an inherent, effortless spread unless resisted.

## Implications

In the translation model, the analyst does not impose pre-given categories but rather follows the actors themselves, allowing their perspectives and practices to shape the account. This approach is descriptive, aiming to account for how order is produced from disorder, rather than assuming it.
