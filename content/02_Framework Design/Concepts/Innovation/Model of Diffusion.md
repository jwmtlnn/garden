The "model of diffusion" is a concept criticized by ANT for its simplified and often misleading representation of how facts, ideas, and technologies spread or fail to spread. In [[@latourScienceActionHow1987]]Part C.) the model of diffusion is contrasted with the [[Model of Interessement]] or [[Translation]]. In the model of diffusion, facts and artefacts are endowed with their own inherent inertia, meaning they seemingly move and spread by themselves, even without the active involvement of [[Actor|actors]]. This invents a form of [[Technological Determinism|determinism]], suggesting that the behavior of actors is caused by the diffusion of facts and machines, rather than acknowledging the active roles of human and non-human actors in shaping them.

Other arguments and examples that [[@latourScienceActionHow1987]]Part C.) makes, are that novelty is explained as the result of "discovery" by some "great initiators" or "geniuses" (e.g., Diesel, Pasteur, Curie) who help pre-existing entities (like microbes or engines) appear in public, rather than recognizing the continuous shaping and transformation by many actors. When facts or innovations fail to spread, the diffusion model explains this by stating that "some groups resist" due to e.g. vested interests, ignorance or even stupidity. These social groups are seen as passive mediums that slow down or absorb the impact of ideas, rather than actively co-producing or transforming them.

ANT scholars argue that the diffusion model is absurd because it ignores the active involvement of a multitude of actors in the making and spreading of scientific facts and technologies. Instead, ANT emphasizes that effects like [[Agency]] and power are collectively generated within [[Actor-Network|heterogeneous networks]] of diverse human and non-human actors.
