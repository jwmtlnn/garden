In the context of innovation systems research, [[@lundvallNationalSystemsInnovation2010]] portrays learning as a multifaceted and crucial process that is fundamental to [[Innovation]], competence building, and economic and societal development. It is not merely an individual cognitive activity but is deeply embedded in social, organizational, and institutional contexts [[@lundvallIntroduction1992]]p.1-2). Learning is considered the most fundamental resource and process in the modern economy, which heads the term of "the learning economy" [[@lundvallLearningEconomyEconomics2016]].

Standard neoclassical [[Perspectives on Economics|economic theory]] is built on the concept of scarcity, focusing on the allocation of a given set of scarce resources. It tends to view technology and innovation as an exogenous process or external variable that falls equally on all perfectly [[Rationality|rational]] and completely informed actors, rather than something emerging from within the economic system [[@panseraInnovationGrowth2025]]. The learning economy perspective posits that knowledge is the most fundamental resource, and learning is the most important process. It focuses on the creation of new kinds of resources, new use values and the capability of an economy to produce and diffuse "use values" with new characteristics. [[@lundvallNationalSystemsInnovation2010]]

> [!quote] [[Bengt-Åke Lundvall]]
> While standard economics tends to regard optimality in the allocation of a given set of use values as the economic problem, _par preference_, we shall focus on the capability of an economy to produce and diffuse _use values with new characteristics_. Moreover, while standard economics takes an atomistic view of the economy, we shall focus on the _systemic interdependence_ between formally independent economic subjects. [[@lundvallLearningEconomyEconomics2016]]p.62).
