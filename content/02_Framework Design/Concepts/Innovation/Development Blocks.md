Development blocks is a concept in the [[Perspectives on Economics|economic theory]] of Erik Dahmén. It is used to understand sequences of evolutionary steps in [[Innovation]] and economic development, particularly how radical innovations create a basis for further adaptive and induced innovations, or to analyze industrial transformation, particularly in the context of business cycles [[@andersenApproachingNationalSystems2010]].

An innovation can be seen as creating the foundation for a series of more or less adaptive decisions and activities over time. This framework combines a Schumpeterian "push" (from radical innovation) with a Schmooklerian "pull" (induced innovations and demand) [[@andersenApproachingNationalSystems2010]]p.90). A core innovation introduces a "jump in the degree of disequilibrium", initiating a sequence of structural tensions and their partial resolution. These tensions often manifest as price and cost signals in various markets, asking for a "gap filling" to address revealed needs or problems in the related chains of production and use, which are also seen as innovative and risky endeavors. [[@andersenApproachingNationalSystems2010]].

From these key insights we can conceive the following ordering:

> [!NOTE] Note
> At the core of a development block analysis is an particular innovation or a cluster of innovations. These innovations create new dependencies and relationships between different parts of the economy, such as new firms, new technologies, and new production processes. As a new innovation takes hold, it often creates imbalances and bottlenecks in other parts of the economy. These structural tensions create pressure for further investment and development to "catch up". The "gap filling" that results from this pressure is what drives the transformation process. Once the complementarities are fully established and the structural tensions are resolved, the block loses its potential for driving further rapid growth.
