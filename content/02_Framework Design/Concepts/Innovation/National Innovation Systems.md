## Introduction & general definition

Systems of Innovation is a concept in understanding how new, economically useful knowledge is produced, [[Model of Diffusion|diffused]], and utilized within an economy. This concept moves beyond viewing [[Innovation]] as isolated events, emphasizing it as an ongoing, ubiquitous, and cumulative process that is deeply rooted to [[Learning]] in the economic structure and institutional setup. [[@lundvallIntroduction1992]][[@johnsonInstitutionalLearning1992]]. The term is considered a concept rather than a general theory, though it is built upon a series of coherent assumptions derived from systematic empirical work [[@lundvallPostScriptInnovation2010]]

## Established definitions

Innovation systems can be understood in a _narrow sense_, which includes organizations directly involved in [[RDI|research]] and [[Learning|exploration]] like R\&D departments, technological institutes, and [[Universities of Applied Sciences|universities]]. In a _broad sense_, it includes all parts and aspects of the economic structure and institutional setup that affect learning, searching, and exploring, such as the production system, marketing system, and financial system. A _National System of Innovation_ (NSI) specifically encompasses these elements and relationships located within or rooted inside the borders of a nation-state. [[@freemanFormalScientificTechnical1992]];[[@lundvallIntroduction1992]].

> [!example] Key concepts of National Systems of Innovation in [[@lundvallNationalSystemsInnovation2010]]
>
> - **Institutions:** formal organizations like firms, universities, government agencies and informal norms, routines, and rules that shape interactions.
> - **[[Learning]]:** innovation is fundamentally a learning process that occurs through interactions between actors in the system.
> - **Interactions and Networks**: relations between participants will be influenced by the specific characteristics of the partners in a number of dimensions.
> - **Differentiation of [[Innovation]]**: The traditional, broad term is differentiated to distinguish between "incremental innovations" and "radical innovations".
> - **[[Development Blocks]]**: drawing on Dahmén's idea, refers to interconnected sequences of innovations and investments in related sectors that form a dynamic process of economic development.

## Critical engagement

The term has been introduced from research into politics. In politics and [[Innovation Policy|policy]] it has been recognized to work as a loose [[Border Object]] rather than well defined and explanatory scientific term, in a similar way that Porters ideas have been used in policymaking. Despite attempts in "mainstream" and "non-mainstream" economics [[@dalumExportSpecialisationStructural1992]];[[@fagerbergHomeMarketHypothesis1992]], it seems that innovation systems cannot be quantitatively compared in a fruitful way: they have to be situated and can't be generalized very well. This makes [[Case Study|case studies]] an important way to gain understanding of innovation systems. [[@miettinenNationalInnovationSystem2002]].
