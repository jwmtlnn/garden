Inscription, as in [[@latourLaboratoryLifeSocial1986]] borrowed from Derrida, refers to a broad range of outputs, traces, marks, symbols, and written or graphic accounts that summarize or embody information about the world. It is considered an operation more fundamental than simple writing; instead "revealing" the "true" representation of the world, the (scientific) knowledge is made real through inscription devices and practices. Inscriptions are material things, and they can be found in various forms such as written words, histograms, spreadsheets, spectra, memos, journals and books [[@callonMappingDynamicsScience1986]]p.10-11.) A core insight is that phenomena, things and realities are thoroughly constituted by the material settings and specifically by inscription devices, which then are not merely tools to observe an independently existing reality; rather, they construct that reality [[@latourLaboratoryLifeSocial1986]].
