An obligatory passage point (OPP) describes a position within a [[Actor-Network|network]] through which other actors must pass to achieve their goal. An OPP can be envisioned as a specific point or node within a network of interconnected actors, where their "threads" converge and pass through. [[@callonElementsSociologyTranslation1986]]p.205-206.)

The concept of OPP also describes the establishment and evolution of power relationships within a network. The actor who controls an OPP holds a position of influence because other actors are dependent on passing through it to further their own interests. The ability of one actor to lock other actors into passing through an OPP is a stage of interessement in the process of [[Translation]]. [[@watsonActorNetworkTheory2007]]p.11-12);[[@callonElementsSociologyTranslation1986]]p.205-211.)

> [!example]
> Researchers in [[@callonElementsSociologyTranslation1986]] attempt to establish their research program as an OPP, arguing that the interests of scallops, scientific colleagues, and fishermen depend on admitting this program.

A successful OPP can often become [[Punctualisation|punctualised]] into a black box. When an actor, set of elements or even the actor-network itself successfully establishes itself as an indispensable point through which others must pass (an OPP), the need to scrutinize its internal complexities and the network that sustains it diminishes. It becomes a stable, unquestioned entity – a black box. [[@latourScienceActionHow1987]]p.139.)
