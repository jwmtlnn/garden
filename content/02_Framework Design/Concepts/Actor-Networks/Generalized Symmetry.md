Generalized symmetry is a central methodological principle, that functions as a strategy to adopt an [[Agnosticism|agnostic]] stance regarding what qualifies as an [[Actor]] in a specific context. This principle advocates also for the symmetrical treatment of both human and nonhuman entities as a priori equals within the analysis. [[@fariasActornetworkTheoryCompanion2020]]p.xxi);[[@callonElementsSociologyTranslation1986]];[[@latourRecallingAnt1999]]. The primary aim of generalized symmetry is to avoid pre-determined assumptions about the nature of actors, resisting the urge to impose a priori distinctions between human intentional action and a material world of causal relations.

> [!quote] [[John Law]]
> To say that there is no fundamental difference between people and objects is **an analytical stance, not an ethical position** [[@lawNotesTheoryActor1992]]p.4).

This demands that analyst refrains from changing their analytical framework when moving between the "technical/natural" and the social aspects of a study. This also means using the same vocabulary or "repertoire" to describe the actions and roles of the actors. [[@callonElementsSociologyTranslation1986]]p.200;221-222.) This does not mean a imperative to treat humans and nonhumans identically in all respects, but rather it suggests that the divisions between them are outcomes of processes, not pre-existing conditions. It serves as a "heuristic flattening" to better understand how diverse elements work together. [[@jacksonAnalyticalMethodologicalUnderstanding2015]]p.38-39);[[@lawAntComplexityNaming1999]].

The objective is to look beyond traditional human-centric perspectives and explore web of relationships involving a [[Heterogeneity|diverse array]] of actants (both human and nonhuman) in the construction of realities. It prompts an analysis that gives due consideration to the agency and contribution of all elements within a network without initial (often hierarchical) assumption.
