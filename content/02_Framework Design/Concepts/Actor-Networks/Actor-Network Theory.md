## Introduction & general definition

_Actor-Network Theory_ (ANT, fin. _toimijaverkkoteoria_) is often described as an approach, sensibility, program or orientation to [[Relationality]], [[Materiality]], [[Heterogeneity]] and process for [[Sociomateriality|sociomaterial]] analysis [[@lawMethodMessSocial2004]]p.157);[[@latourRecallingAnt1999]]. It aims for empirical descriptions of the relational ties and sociomaterial flux and it's [[Translation|translations]] in a [[Actor-Network|network]] [[@latourReassemblingSocialIntroduction2005]]. The unit of analysis is often process or association [[@miettinenRiddleThingsActivity1999]]p.174). Despite its name, whether it truly is a theory is debatable and there is even an a-theoretical nature to it [[@latourReassemblingSocialIntroduction2005]].

## Historical context

ANT has its roots in the Centre de Sociologie de l'Innovation (CSI) of the Mines de Paris and emerged from the field of Science and Technology Studies (STS) in the early 1980s. It was/is often referred also to as "a sociology of translations" or "a sociology of associations" before the term Actor-Network Theory was coined. [[@akrichActorNetworkTheory2023]];[[@callonActorNetworkTheory2001]]. Foundational scholars are considered to be [[Bruno Latour]], [[Madeleine Akrich]], [[John Law]] and [[Michel Callon]]. Other influential, but not at all peripheral, are e.g. [[Annemarie Mol]], Steve Woolgar, Karin Knorr-Cetina and [[Donna Haraway]]. In the context of [[National Innovation Systems|Innovation Systems]], has [[Reijo Miettinen]] examined the applicability of ANT since the 1990s. As previously stated, it is well used, developed and debated in social sciences, but has been "spread" over various other fields and uses [[@lawAntComplexityNaming1999]]p.10).

In the field of [[Innovation Management]] have [[@sieklickiDescriptionActionActor2021]] recognized in their review three somewhat different literature streams of actor-network theory: descriptive, proactive managerial and proactive collaborative. The descriptive stream stays strictly symmetrical, where the latter two somewhat break the symmetry in order to enable actionable insights.

## Established definitions

ANT has evolved through the decades and because it lacks a theoretical unity it might be difficult to define [[@miettinenRiddleThingsActivity1999]]p.171). There is no generic way to apply ANT because there is no clear "methodological prescription", but there are many key elements and choices that persist in it's intellectual practice [[@jacksonAnalyticalMethodologicalUnderstanding2015]] or show up in contemporary ANT "and After".

From these key concepts we can form the following ordering:

> [!NOTE]
> Actor-Network Theory views reality as constructed through dynamic networks of diverse actors, both human and nonhuman, treating them with generalized symmetry and ontological agnosticism. These actor-networks, characterized by heterogeneity and hybridity, are enacted through processes of translation, where e.g. inscriptions and quasi-objects facilitate interactions via intermediaries and mediators. Through enrolment, actants are integrated, and often networks have obligatory passage points where all actors must converge. Immutable mobiles and objects stabilize and disseminate influence, revealing the multiplicity inherent in social and material realities. Complex networks are often simplified into punctualised black boxes, which the ANT tries to provide a view into.

ANT does not use "[[Society]]" or "social" as explanations in its analysis, but instead tries to explain them "in the making" as an ongoing achievement. It aims not to reproduce the classical oppositions or dualisms of nature/culture, micro/macro, agency/culture etc. or to see facts mirroring nature, but to show how they are constructed and to provide tools for analyzing that process. [[@callonActorNetworkTheory2001]]. It is thus a constructionist, post-structuralist, [[Material-Semiotic]] and an anti-essentialist approach. In the context of [[Innovation]] processes, ANT is commonly used to study them as a simultaneous development of an [[Artefact]] and a network of [[Actor|actors]] connected to it [[@miettinenRiddleThingsActivity1999]]p.170).

## Adjacent approaches

In many ways a similar approach called the Social Construction of Technology (SCOT, foundational authors being [[Wiebe Bijker]] & Trevor Pinch) can not be accounted as ANT, according to [[@latourReassemblingSocialIntroduction2005]]p. 10-11), since in it the "_the social is kept stable all along and accounts for the shape of technological change._" Another similarly kept approach is the [[Activity Theory|Cultural Historical Activity Theory]] (AT or CHAT), which has a different disciplinary and philosophical background, but shares many methodological grounds [[@miettinenRiddleThingsActivity1999]]. A closely related and broader body of thought is [[New Materialism]], which it shares with e.g. the "flat" [[Ontology]] and in some sense ANT (and perhaps also AT) can be seen as a contribution to it [[@kampActorNetworkTheory2019]].
