## Punctualisation or Black Boxes

Punctualisation refers to the simplificatory effects when [[Actor-Network|network]] patterns are widely performed and can be more or less taken for granted [[@lawNotesTheoryActor1992]]p.5). These punctualised networks are sometimes referred to by some as **black boxes**. The word is borrowed from cybernetics, where for example a piece of machinery or software becomes too complex to describe, so it's reduced to a "box in the chart" with inputs and outputs [[@latourScienceActionHow1987]]p.2-3).

When everything goes well, it can appear as if black boxes are effortlessly moving through space, durable by their own inner strength, leading to illusions of "irreversible progress of science" or "irresistible power of technology" - inertia [[@latourScienceActionHow1987]]p.132). This represents "ready-made science" or technology, often described in terms of [[Translation#Translation vs. Diffusion (Transportation)|diffusion]].

However, this appearance masks the labor involved in building and maintaining the network, so black boxes are more like "lashups of [[Heterogeneity|heterogeneous]] bits and pieces" [[@lawAntComplexityNaming1999]]p.216-217). Their stability is not written in the stone either, they face resistance and may as well fail [[@lawNotesTheoryActor1992]]p.5).

A successful black box acts also as an [[Obligatory Passage Point]], holding together different systems of alliances [[@latourScienceActionHow1987]]p.139):

> [!quote] [[Bruno Latour]]
> This is why we call such black boxes 'hard facts', or 'highly sophisticated machines', or 'powerful theories', or 'indisputable evidence'. All these adjectives that allude to strength and power rightly point out the disproportionate number of associations gathered in these black boxes, so disproportionate indeed that they are what keep the multitude of allies in place. [[@latourScienceActionHow1987]]p.139).
