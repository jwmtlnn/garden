> [!abstract] Abstract \[en]
>
> Actor-networks do not enter computational systems unchanged. They are translated through documents, parsers, chunks, prompts, entity types, consolidation rules, graph databases, and researcher judgments. This paper treats that chain of technical operations as a methodological problem for computational Actor-Network Theory (ANT). Four decades after _Leximappe_ introduced co-word mapping into the sociology of translation, computational methods still face a predicate bottleneck: lexical co-occurrence reveals which terms appear together, but not how heterogeneous entities enable, constrain, translate, and contest one another.
>
> This methodological preprint introduces a pilot configuration of LightRAG for ANT and praxiography using an extraction profile (`ant_ontology.yml`). The profile classifies entities into seven substantive pragmatic forms plus a residual category while treating terms such as mediator, spokesperson, obligatory passage point, and black box as passage-specific relational roles. A description-before-characterization constraint requires the model to preserve concrete actions, artifacts, quantities, constraints, and consequences before optionally introducing ANT infralanguage. Generated graph relationships and descriptions are treated as navigational summaries requiring verification against contributing source passages.
>
> A diagnostic evaluation compares LightRAG’s default extraction with the symmetrical profile using Arthur Conan Doyle’s _The Adventure of Silver Blaze_ (1894) and NASA’s publicly released 32-file Kennedy Space Center email collection concerning Columbia STS-107 (2003). The results are corpus-dependent: the symmetrical profile redistributes attention toward inscriptions and calculation tools in the technical archive but produces less topological change in the literary corpus. It preserves selected material actions but struggles to connect a non-event across passages when a key negative clue, the watchdog that did not bark, is first expressed elliptically and explained only later. Provenance auditing also shows that recipient metadata alone cannot warrant claims about an individual’s organizational action, while entity-resolution frictions expose the boundary between mechanical duplication and praxiographic multiplicity. Treated as a “Thick Machine” (Munk, Olesen & Jacomy, 2022), the pipeline does not automate thick description; instead, it helps locate mundane non-human actants while its naming, classification, and consolidation failures direct the researcher back to source passages requiring close explication. The pilot evaluates the indexing and graph-construction phase; retrieval performance is outside its scope.

Abordé comme une « _Thick Machine_ » (Munk, Olesen et Jacomy, 2022), le dispositif n’automatise pas la description dense (_thick description_) : il facilite le repérage d’actants non humains ordinaires tandis que ses échecs de nomination, de classification et de consolidation orientent le chercheur vers les passages d’origine nécessitant une explication approfondie. ## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 The Lineage

Organizational projects are often assembled through documents and technical tools as much as through meetings and executive decisions. Budgets allocate responsibility across partners, mathematical models turn physical observations into risk estimates, email recipient lists define information circulation, and funding or safety rules determine which proposals and actions can proceed. In large digital archives, these relations are difficult to follow across files, formats, and versions. Language models and knowledge graphs offer a way to trace these connections at scale, yet they also introduce their own classifications, consolidations, and omissions. This paper examines how organizational records are transformed when a language model converts them into a graph of entities and relationships, asking what becomes visible, how associations are described, how repeated mentions are merged, and how researchers can audit the resulting graph against original source texts.

Early studies of translation networks pioneered co-word analysis as a means of tracing techno-scientific controversies without relying on a priori social categories [[@callonTranslationsProblematicNetworks1983]]; [[@callonMappingDynamicsScience1986]]; [[@callonCowordAnalysisTool1991]]. The method was subsequently debated in scientometrics [[@leydesdorffValidationStudyLEXIMAPPE1992]]; [[@courtialCommentsLeydesdorffsValidation1992]]. Drawing on foundational arguments that actors build durable arrangements by enrolling diverse human and non-human elements [[@callonUnscrewingBigLeviathan1981]]; [[@latourScienceActionHow1987]], co-word analysis treated scientific texts as collections of inscriptions that link practitioners, instruments, biological substances, and institutional initiatives. This analytical lineage informed second-generation digital methods platforms, such as the widely-used _CorTexT_ platform [[@cortext_manager_v2_bibtex]], which extended lexical co-occurrence mapping across longitudinal, multi-source scientific and public corpora.

Despite its foundational status, classic co-word analysis and early natural language processing (NLP) suffered from an important methodological limitation when evaluated against ANT’s requirement to trace heterogeneous actors and the relations through which they modify one another. Operationalizing its principle of generalized symmetry through flat keyword co-occurrence matrices strips away the verbs and relational predicates that specify _how_ actants translate and modify one another. Modeling a document as an unstructured bag of co-occurring terms conflates passive co-presence with active mediation. It reduces experimental apparatuses, statistical formulas, and regulatory bodies to undifferentiated dots in a network projection without capturing the qualitative roles they perform within a text.

To overcome the limited predicate information of co-word graphs, computational and social science scholars have sought productive exchange between qualitative theories of distributed agency and relational computational linguistics. Recent developments in machine learning and language modeling make it possible to extract relational predicates and source-target fields rather than flat term co-occurrences. For example, [[@pournakiExtractingNarrativeSignals2024]] use language-model-assisted narrative extraction to construct directed networks from public discourse. I anchor this paper in recent digital STS work on machine learning and artificial intelligence [[@venturiniActorNetworkNetworkAnalysis2019]]; [[@munkThickMachineAnthropological2022]]; [[@madsenFrictionMachineHow2023]]. Whereas Venturini, Munk & Jacomy (2019) establish the topological requirements that a computational graph must satisfy to remain accountable to Actor-Network Theory, Munk, Olesen & Jacomy (2022) establish the epistemological posture for engaging the model that constructs that graph. Reworking Clifford Geertz’s distinction between mechanistic _explanation_ and interpretive _explication_, they propose treating machine-learning models as "Thick Machines". The machine is simultaneously "thick" in the colloquial sense of missing situated cultural nuance and "thick" in the Geertzian sense of pointing toward _thick description_, as their unexpected graph structures and extraction failures send the researcher back to the original source texts where close explication is needed.

### 1.2 Retrieval-Augmented Generation

While LLMs extract and summarize individual passages well, applying them directly to organizational fieldwork archives faces two practical limits: relevant interactions are scattered across many files and formats, and pasting an entire document repository into a single prompt either exceeds the model's context window or causes it to miss details buried in the middle. Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) addresses this by separating document storage from generation. In a standard vector-based (or "naive") RAG workflow, source documents are split into fixed-length text segments (called _chunks_, typically 500 to 2 000 tokens long) and converted by an embedding model into numerical vectors that represent semantic similarity. When a researcher asks a question, the system retrieves the chunks whose vectors are closest to the query and inserts those excerpts into the LLM's prompt to produce a source-cited answer.

However, for many purposes, but also for ANT and praxiographic inquiry, standard vector RAG suffers from chunk isolation. Because documents are divided into separate text segments and retrieved by semantic similarity alone, standard RAG struggles to follow multi-step sociomaterial chains across files. Connecting a budget formula in an Excel sheet to a contested slide deck and an informal chat negotiation in Teams is difficult when those documents share few common terms.

Graph-based RAG architectures, most notably Microsoft GraphRAG [[@edgeLocalGlobalGraph2024]] and LightRAG [[@guoLightRAGSimpleFast2024]], address chunk isolation by introducing an LLM-extracted knowledge graph construction layer between the raw text chunks and the retrieval engine. For qualitative methodology, deploying the LightRAG framework yields two distinct, complementary analytical products corresponding to its two operational phases. The first product is a standalone Property Graph Database generated during the indexing phase. As the LLM reads each text chunk to extract entities (nodes) and their interrelations (edges), it compiles a persistent graph database (such as Neo4j or NetworkX). Repeatedly extracted entities are consolidated under model-generated identifier strings stored in the `entity_id` property. Even before querying the system in natural language, the researcher can inspect this database directly, for example through Visual Network Analysis [[@venturiniVisualNetworkAnalysis2015]] or Cypher queries, to examine degree distributions, spot candidate obligatory passage points, and read relationship descriptions linked to their source chunks. The second product is a conversational query interface used during the retrieval phase. Rather than relying on simple chunk similarity, LightRAG translates the researcher's question into both specific entity names and broader relational themes, traverses the knowledge graph to gather relevant nodes, edges, and their originating verbatim text chunks, and synthesizes a provenance-linked response.

Yet out-of-the-box graph-based RAG still presents a risk of taxonomic and relational flattening. LightRAG v1.5.6's default extraction prompt combines four categories inherited from standard Named Entity Recognition (`person`, `organization`, `location`, and `event`) plus seven broad noun buckets (`artifact`, `concept`, `content`, `data`, `method`, `creature`, and `naturalobject`). While this schema recognizes non-human objects, it does not distinguish them according to how they act in a source passage. Nor does it prevent mediation, obligatory passage point (OPP) status, or black-boxing from being treated as static node properties rather than situated relational achievements.

When applied to organizational fieldwork, this taxonomic classifier may also privilege people and formal organizations while lumping consequential non-human traces into generic buckets. In practice, spreadsheet macros, mathematical risk models, damage-assessment briefings, project budgets, portal character limits, and funding or safety rules do more than sit in the background. They recalculate partner commitments, constrain what can be proposed, and operate as active mediators or OPPs in trials of strength. This under-differentiation is particularly limiting for studies grounded in Annemarie Mol's praxiography [[@molBodyMultipleOntology2002]], which examines how objects and realities are multiply enacted through concrete, situated socio-material practices.  For example, consider a Columbia space mission's email that lists Linda Ham among its recipients. A graph extractor can validly create a node for Ham and link it to the message, yet that edge records documentary circulation rather than organizational action. Technical elements in the same archive, including Crater model's equations and foam-debris estimates, participate directly in engineering assessments while possibly receiving less stable names across files. Distinguishing documentary presence, graph connection, and warranted organizational action motivates the configuration developed below.

### 1.3 Scope of the Contribution

The development of this apparatus is motivated by my ongoing doctoral study investigating the pre-award preparation of RESILAKE, a 12-partner Horizon Europe research, development, and innovation (RDI) consortium proposal comprising largely of Microsoft Teams chat logs, meeting transcripts, email threads, proposal drafts, spreadsheets and other documents. This paper’s primary contribution is the combination of LLM/RAG technologies with actor-network and praxiographic inquiry by presenting a configuration pattern for pragmatic passage-level typing, description before characterization, and provenance auditing with LightRAG.

> [!info] Box 1: Bridging vocabulary
>
> - _(Source) passage and chunk:_ A contiguous excerpt of text serving as the local unit of qualitative evidence. During indexing, LightRAG operationalizes passages as configured-length text _chunks_.
> - _Entity/node & relationship/edge:_ The extractor represents a named entity as a graph _node_ (`entity_id`) and an extracted association between two entities as an _edge_.
> - _Inscription:_ A textual, numerical, visual, or material trace that transports, compares, or transforms claims across sites.
> - _Enactment & multiplicity:_ Objects and realities are produced (_enacted_) in situated socio-material practices. Enacted across different practices, an object is "_more than one, and less than many_": its enactments differ across sites while overlapping and hanging together [[@lawAircraftStoriesDecentering2002]]; [[@molBodyMultipleOntology2002]].
> - _Mediator vs. intermediary:_ An _intermediary_ transports meaning or force without changing it; a _mediator_ transforms, translates, or modifies the claims, capacities, or actions passing through it [[@latourReassemblingSocialIntroduction2005]].
> - _Punctualization:_ The situated representation of a heterogeneous web of actors and artifacts as a single acting unit (such as "NASA", "the Mission Management Team", or "the project").
> - _Obligatory passage point (OPP):_ A situated condition, artifact, or actor that others must pass through or satisfy to pursue an identified goal [[@callonElementsSociologyTranslation1986]]. Here "_passage_" denotes traversing a relational bottleneck rather than a text excerpt.
> - _Infralanguage:_ Minimalist relational terms (such as _mediator_, _spokesperson_, or _trial of strength_) used in ANT to trace how actors associate without replacing their concrete actions with preset social categories [[@latourReassemblingSocialIntroduction2005]].
> - _Thick description & the Thick Machine:_ In interpretive anthropology, _thick description_ reconstructs the layered contexts through which an action or trace becomes meaningful. ANT similarly insists on following concrete associations without replacing them prematurely with external social explanations. A _Thick Machine_ [[@munkThickMachineAnthropological2022]] does not write thick descriptions autonomously; its extraction patterns and consolidation frictions point the researcher toward empirical passages requiring close human explication.
> - _Consolidation and description merging:_ LightRAG's indexing mechanic that combines repeated node identifiers (`entity_id`) and repeated canonically ordered endpoint pairs across chunks, retaining source references and passage-specific descriptions separated by `<SEP>` until a configured summarization condition invokes an internal LLM prompt.
> - _Provenance:_ Stored `source_id` metadata linking graph nodes and relationships to the verbatim source passages (chunks) that contributed to their extraction.

Without modifying LightRAG's core codebase, I load an external extraction profile (`ant_ontology.yml`, reproduced in Appendix B) through the program's native `ENTITY_TYPE_PROMPT_FILE` setting and run the indexing pipeline locally with open-weight models. The filename `ant_ontology.yml` is retained as a technical identifier; analytically, it functions as a pragmatic extraction profile rather than trying to establish a formal ontology. The profile assigns entities pragmatic forms while keeping ANT roles tied to particular passages. It requires a concrete description before ANT terminology or infralanguage is introduced. It also distinguishes technical platforms from the sites, episodes, inscriptions, and quantified commitments produced through them.

Rather than expecting an LLM to reconstruct an actor-network on its own, I approach the configured pipeline as a "Thick Machine" [[@munkThickMachineAnthropological2022]] that makes heterogeneous associations easier to inspect while keeping its own extraction and consolidation choices visible. The resulting graph is approached as an auditable index rather than an autonomous interpretation. The pilot evaluates LightRAG's first operating phase and product only: entity and relationship extraction, name-based consolidation, description merging, provenance retention, and the resulting graph database topology. It does not evaluate the second product or operational mode: conversational retrieval. Instead, the indexing layer is examined as a prerequisite that determines what kind of evidence becomes available to downstream retrieval.

This study approaches the topic with three methodological research questions:

- RQ1 (What becomes visible): How does the custom extraction profile change which human and non-human entities are categorized and made central in the graph compared with LightRAG's defaults?
- RQ2 (How relations are described): What kinds of concrete relationships and blind spots emerge when the model must describe passage-level actions before applying ANT terms?
- RQ3 (How consolidation shapes the graph): How do LightRAG's name-merging and summarization rules alter the network, and when do those mechanics expose meaningful ambiguity in the source texts?

To provide a legally open two-corpus diagnostic while the raw RESILAKE archive undergoes pre-ingestion pseudonymization, I evaluate this pilot apparatus across an openly accessible literary corpus and a publicly released organizational archive of manageable scale that have established analytical lineages in epistemology and Science and Technology Studies. First, Arthur Conan Doyle's classic Sherlock Holmes detective narrative _The Adventure of Silver Blaze_ [[@doyleAdventureSilverBlaze1894]] serves as a bounded test for abductive trace epistemology (cf. [[@ginzburgMorelliFreudSherlock1980]]; [[@ecoSignThreeDupin1988]]; [[@latourAramisLoveTechnology1996]]). The second corpus comprises 32 unique files from NASA’s publicly released 33-file Kennedy Space Center employee email FOIA subset [[@nasaKennedyEmployeeEMails2003]]. This single-centre organizational communication archive provides a limited structural analogue (cf. [[@tufteCognitiveStylePowerPoint2004]]; [[@starbuckOrganizationLimitLessons2005]]; [[@vaughanSystemEffectsSlippages2005]]) for testing the pipeline before application to multi-partner RDI records. The public release contains 33 files. One duplicate was excluded, leaving 32 unique files in the ingested corpus.

The pilot evaluation yields four main findings for qualitative organizational research. First, changing the extraction instructions alters the things made visible in the graph. This manifests as shifting attention in the NASA archive toward technical briefings, prediction models, and material constraints while also surfacing archival formatting artifacts. Second, a topological connection in the graph does not by itself document organizational action, as demonstrated by auditing the Linda Ham email-recipient link against its source passages. Third, automated naming rules split a single working group across multiple casing variants, while LightRAG’s threshold-triggered internal summarization creates a separate architectural risk of compressing episodic actions into an atemporal profile. Fourth, some apparent entity node duplication preserves analytically meaningful differences, such as the distinction between observed physical foam debris, competing dimensional estimates, and specific model inputs.

## 2. From Records to Graphs

### 2.1 Why a Formal Graph Is Not an Actor-Network

To justify graph-based RAG as a methodological apparatus for Digital STS, it is first necessary to address the epistemological tension between formal graph mathematics and Actor-Network Theory. The discussion in _"Actor-Network vs. Network Analysis vs. Digital Networks: Are We Talking about the Same Networks?"_ [[@venturiniActorNetworkNetworkAnalysis2019]] points out that digital networks analyzed through conventional Social Network Analysis (SNA) differ from Latourian actor-networks in four ways. First, regarding _partiality and media translation_, digital traces are never neutral reflections of social interaction, but partial inscriptions formatted by the technical affordances of their host media. Second, regarding _node and edge uniformity versus radical heterogeneity_, mathematical graphs standardize vertices and collapse multiplex, contradictory, or antagonistic interactions into simple numerical edge weights, whereas ANT demands ontological heterogeneity across nodes and qualitative specificity across edges. Third, standard graph theory struggles with _actor-network reversibility_ (Actor = Network) because it generally fixes the boundary between atomic nodes and global network structure prior to calculation, contrasting with the principle that any actor (such as an RDI consortium or a mission management board) is simultaneously a punctualized node and an unfolded network of actants [[@callonUnscrewingBigLeviathan1981]]; [[@latourNetworksSocietiesSpheres2011]]. Fourth, conventional SNA models _transport rather than translation_, measuring flow across fixed structural ties (through degree, betweenness, and brokerage), whereas ANT traces how actors and relationships change one another as they interact.

### 2.2 LightRAG & Microsoft GraphRAG

Graph-based RAG frameworks such as Microsoft GraphRAG [[@edgeLocalGlobalGraph2024]] and LightRAG [[@guoLightRAGSimpleFast2024]] both construct entity-relationship representations linked to source text units. Microsoft GraphRAG additionally partitions the graph into hierarchical communities and pre-generates community reports for corpus-level thematic retrieval. LightRAG was selected for this pilot because the research questions concern incremental indexing, direct inspection of extracted nodes and relationships, external prompt-profile configuration, and persistence in Neo4j without requiring a precomputed community-report hierarchy. The choice reflects these methodological priorities rather than a general claim of architectural superiority (Box 2).

During indexing, each extracted entity is stored under its `entity_id` string with a contextual prose description, while each extracted relationship receives a prose description and structured keywords. When an entity identifier or canonically ordered endpoint pair recurs across chunks, LightRAG merges the records, retains `source_id` links to the contributing passages, and either preserves the accumulated descriptions separated by `<SEP>` or condenses them once they reach a configured summarization threshold. New documents can be added incrementally without rebuilding a corpus-wide community hierarchy.

When configured for symmetrical inquiry, the resulting graph supports two ANT-oriented analytical procedures without automating either of them. First, relationship descriptions allow the analyst to examine whether a passage depicts transport without observable transformation or an association that modifies actors, inscriptions, capacities, or possible actions. _Intermediary_ and _mediator_ [[@latourReassemblingSocialIntroduction2005]] therefore remain passage-grounded analytical characterizations rather than properties inferred from topology alone. Second, topology and relationship keywords can surface possible OPPs [[@callonMappingDynamicsScience1986]]; [[@callonElementsSociologyTranslation1986]]. A highly connected `RuleOrRequirement`, `Inscription`, or `TechnicalArtifact` remains only an OPP candidate. Its situated OPP status requires returning through the stored provenance references to establish that actors pursuing an identifiable goal had to pass through, satisfy, or align with it, while also examining bypasses and counter-translations.

These operations do not produce a neutral representation of a pre-existing actor-network. For example, chunk boundaries limit which associations appear together in a single pass; the extraction prompt governs which traces and verbs receive attention; model-generated identifier strings control node merging; and threshold summarization can compress episodic actions into generalized profiles. At the same time, shared `source_id` pointers allow the researcher to trace any consolidated node or edge back to the source passages from which it was assembled.

## 3. Configuring the Apparatus for Symmetrical Inquiry

### 3.1 Making Extraction Choices

LightRAG allows its extraction instructions to be loaded from an external YAML file. I use this mechanism to configure the generic indexing pipeline for praxiographic inquiry without maintaining a separate software fork. In the tested deployment, `ENTITY_TYPE_PROMPT_FILE` loads `ant_ontology.yml` (Appendix B) from the prompt directory at server startup, and this profile overrides LightRAG's default entity guidance. Following the digital STS proposition that designing a computational tool entails making explicit analytical choices [[@venturiniActorNetworkNetworkAnalysis2019]]; [[@munkThickMachineAnthropological2022]], isolating the extraction instructions in a standalone YAML file treats the schema as a reflexive theoretical memo rather than an invisible software default. Adapting LightRAG's indexing pipeline without modifying its core codebase rests on two analytical compromises.

The first compromise is _separating pragmatic empirical form from situated relational roles_. The extraction profile does not claim to formalize ANT as a fixed taxonomy of ontological essences. In strict ANT and praxiography, concepts such as _mediator_, _intermediary_, _spokesperson_, _obligatory passage point_, and _black box_ are temporary, relational achievements enacted within specific episodes of practice [[@callonElementsSociologyTranslation1986]]; [[@molBodyMultipleOntology2002]]; [[@latourReassemblingSocialIntroduction2005]]. Vanilla LightRAG, however, permits only a single type string per node (`name`, `type`, and `description` in the extraction schema, stored in Neo4j under `entity_id`, `entity_type`, and `description`). The profile therefore classifies entities into seven substantive pragmatic node types based on their empirically visible form, plus a residual `Other` category (Table 1). Relational roles such as mediation, OPP status, spokespersonship, and translation are recorded instead in passage-grounded `description` fields and relationship `keywords`.

Separating visible form from situated role reduces, though does not eliminate, classificatory stabilization, since deciding whether a trace is primarily a person, inscription, technical artifact, or site remains an analytical choice. Two categories carry explicit passage-level evidence thresholds to minimize premature reification. `PunctualizedCollective` marks passages that attribute a unified name, decision, output, or spokesperson position to a heterogeneous group, rather than assuming that organizations are internally unified. Similarly, `QuantifiedCommitment` requires passage evidence that e.g. a quantified allocation, person-month figure, budget amount, overhead rate, capacity claim, cost item, or contested asset is proposed, attributed, negotiated, accepted, rejected, or treated as binding.

The second compromise concerns LightRAG’s consolidation of repeated nodes and relationships. Records sharing an identical entity identifier (`entity_id`) or canonically ordered endpoint pair accumulate `source_id` references and passage-specific descriptions, so empirical agency is carried by the stored descriptions rather than by endpoint order. Records with one contributing description retain a single description; those with two to seven descriptions retain them separated by `<SEP>`. When the configured summarization condition is reached, LightRAG invokes an internal description-summarization prompt to rewrite the accumulated descriptions into a synthesized paragraph. Because this internal summarization step operates outside the extraction guidance in `ant_ontology.yml` and can compress episodic or contradictory relations into an atemporal profile, merged descriptions are treated as navigational summaries whose claims must be verified through stored provenance references against the contributing verbatim passages.

Beyond pragmatic node typing, the `entity_types_guidance` block in `ant_ontology.yml` governs how the language model profiles nodes and edges through a description-before-characterization constraint. In ANT, thick description requires tracing concrete socio-material associations closely enough that they do not need to be supplemented by external structural explanations [[@latourReassemblingSocialIntroduction2005]]. Source passages are already partial inscriptions shaped by host platforms, file formats, chunking, and prompt instructions, and a language model operating through statistical "dumb meaning" [[@bajohrDumbMeaningMachine2023]] over isolated chunks cannot write an ethnographic thick description. To prevent the model from overwriting empirical specifics with sociological buzzwords, the profile requires the extractor to first write a passage-grounded account of what the entity does or changes in the passage, preserving specific actors, artifacts, versions, quantities, constraints, and consequences before adding lightweight ANT infralanguage when warranted by those consequences. If a passage supports only this concrete account, the model stops there, enacting “grounding as deflation” [[@ficozziGroundingDeflationTurning2026]] and preserving the concrete traces from which the researcher constructs a thick description across documents.

Stored provenance links support passage-level auditing of its records, but they do not expose the model’s internal reasoning or transfer interpretive responsibility from the researcher. Using the graph database as a navigational layer aligns with recent calls for two-way transparency between AI outputs and original qualitative material [[@schildtCaseArtificialIntelligence2026]], although such provenance still does not open the model’s internal black box. It provides a bounded form of output accountability, while interpretive responsibility remains with the human researcher [[@vesaArtificialIntelligenceRationalized2022]].

To capture praxiographic multiplicity in digital environments without fragmenting the graph, the profile also establishes a multi-scale representation of digital platforms, settings, and episodes. Digital environments (such as Microsoft Teams or email systems) are neither neutral containers nor single monolithic actors; their apparent unity is itself a punctualization across diverse practices. To prevent LightRAG's name-based consolidation from either collapsing all digital interactions into one undifferentiated platform vertex or splintering the graph into theoretical duplicates (`Teams-as-platform`, `Teams-as-archive`), the profile's type-precedence and canonical-naming rules separate five analytical scales:

1. _Technical platforms and models_ (`Microsoft Teams` or the `Crater` mathematical tool, typed as `TechnicalArtifact`) configure access, calculation, persistence, and transcription.
2. _Persistent settings_ (`RESILAKE Budget Channel` or an `STS-107` email briefing thread, typed as `SiteOfPractice`) organize ongoing communication and document storage on those platforms.
3. _Bounded practice episodes_ (`March Budget Reconciliation Meeting`, a `SPAT Board Meeting`, or an `MMT Meeting`, typed as `SiteOfPractice`) take place within those settings.
4. _Inscriptions and quantified commitments_ (`Consortium_Budget_Matrix_v04.xlsx` or Boeing's damage-assessment briefing) are generated and revised during those episodes.
5. _Provisionally stabilized arrangements_ (such as `the project` when represented as an acting unit under `PunctualizedCollective`, or a routine `turnaround issue` typed as `Other` with its stabilization recorded in descriptions and edge keywords) emerge when those inscriptions coordinate competing enactments across sites.

The profile asks the model to state the concrete action first, such as recording, revising, recalculating, authorizing, or excluding, before adding a translation label. It permits passage-level descriptions of stabilization or black-boxing only where local consequences are explicit in the chunk. Across documents and versions, the researcher then traces corpus-level signs of stabilization: disappearing alternatives, movement from draft to approved status, superseded files, and convergence on an authorized assessment or budget. Because structured JSON extraction was enabled (`ENTITY_EXTRACTION_USE_JSON=true`), the reported runs supplied the model with the abstract placeholder template in Appendix B (`entity_extraction_json_examples`) rather than the worked Horizon Europe example (`entity_extraction_examples`). The model therefore relied on the category definitions, guidelines, and field placeholders without an in-context demonstration, which helps account for the naming variation and residual classifications observed in Section 4.

### 3.2 Ingestion and Document Parsing

Slicing heterogeneous organizational records, such as multi-section investigation reports and threaded email chains, into chunks risks separating local statements from their broader documentary context. To retain structural context where document hierarchies are available, the ingestion configuration enables hierarchical heading-path injection (`ENABLE_CONTENT_HEADINGS=true`). This feature is designed to track parent headings during segmentation and append the resulting breadcrumb path to each chunk.

_The Adventure of Silver Blaze_ [[@doyleAdventureSilverBlaze1894]] was retrieved from Project Gutenberg and ingested as a plain-text `.txt` file. During ingestion, LightRAG passes files through format-specific document parsers (software modules that read incoming files and extract content). Markdown and plain-text inputs were processed by general text parsers with a legacy fallback. Because the short _Silver Blaze_ file consisted of continuous plain text with only an opening section label (`I. Silver Blaze`) and no formal heading markup, the parser detected no section hierarchy to attach as context paths to the resulting chunks.

The Columbia STS-107 collection presented a different problem. Its legacy raster PDFs were pre-processed with Adobe Acrobat’s _Scan & OCR_ function in Searchable Image mode, which placed searchable text layers behind the scanned pages. This allowed LightRAG to ingest OCR-derived text without runtime vision-language-model inference in this pilot. Several PDFs nevertheless contained presentation slides, tables, plotted graphs, diagrams, and other layout-dependent inscriptions. Optical Character Recognition made visible labels, numbers, and text fragments from these elements available to the text-extraction pipeline, but it does not reliably reconstruct their spatial organization, graphical relationships, or intended reading order. Heading breadcrumbs were consequently available only where the parser recovered a usable textual hierarchy. They should not then be understood as a uniform reconstruction of the PDFs’ documentary or visual structure. Relations conveyed primarily through visual proximity, arrows, axes, colour, or slide layout may therefore be absent or fragmented in the extracted graph even when their textual labels were recovered.

### 3.3 Execution Environment and Data Governance

The pilot ran locally on Lapland University of Applied Sciences' institutional infrastructure using open-weight language and embedding models `gpt-oss-120b` [[@openaiGptoss120bGptoss20bModel2025]] and `qwen3-embedding-8b` [[@zhangQwen3EmbeddingAdvancing2025]]. One NVIDIA A100 (80 GB) GPU was allocated for the task. Local execution was chosen to develop the workflow under the data-protection and security conditions required for the later RESILAKE corpus. Everything but the Neo4j databases resided in a Cryptomator vault. Appendix A reports the configuration in more detail.

## 4. Exploratory Pilot Evaluation

### 4.1 Overview

Using an LLM-constructed knowledge graph in Actor-Network Theory and praxiography requires a clear baseline for evaluation. Recent Computational Grounded Theory workflows [[@nelsonComputationalGroundedTheory2020]] and their LLM extensions, including LOGOS [[@piLOGOSLLMdrivenEndtoEnd2025]], Neo-Grounded Theory [[@wenNeoGroundedTheoryMethodological2025]], and Human-in-the-Loop CGT [[@alqazlanNovelHumanintheLoopComputational2025]], typically validate machine outputs by measuring inter-coder reliability against human annotators. Yet an experiment comparing ten qualitative researchers with LLMs [[@moatsMeetingMachinesHalfway2026]] shows how mechanical-agreement benchmarks can become artificial when applied to ethnographic coding. Drawing on Aaron Cicourel, the authors argue that achieving high inter-coder reliability forces human researchers to "meet the machines halfway" by setting aside contextual judgment, irony, and institutional knowledge in favor of formalist rules.

In this "Thick Machine" [[@munkThickMachineAnthropological2022]] approach, the division of labor between computation and thick description is explicit: the pipeline indexes candidate associations and flags ambiguities, while the human researcher performs the interpretive explication through two kinds of analytical friction [[@madsenFrictionMachineHow2023]]. First, it produces _emic friction_ by unsettling initial expectations about the empirical material. Researchers can overlook routine technical models, documents, and procedural rules. Instructing the extractor to attend to `Inscription`, `TechnicalArtifact`, `RuleOrRequirement`, and `QuantifiedCommitment` entities makes these non-human participants visible in the graph and highlights where an object or problem is enacted differently across passages [[@molBodyMultipleOntology2002]]; [[@madsenFrictionMachineHow2023]]. Second, the system produces _etic friction_ by testing the researcher's own categories and boundaries between signal and noise [[@madsenFrictionMachineHow2023]]. When the model assigns material clues to `Other`, splits an actor across name variants, merges distinct episodes into a timeless summary, or elevates file-conversion artifacts into central hubs, those breakdowns slow down analysis and force a closer look at the source passages.

Description before characterization should not be confused with thick description. The extraction constraint preserves local actors, artifacts, quantities, actions, and consequences, but a longer or more detailed graph description is not thereby ethnographically thick. Thick description is produced when the researcher reconstructs how a trace becomes meaningful within a situated episode, across documentary forms, institutional histories, practical concerns, and competing enactments. In the Thick Machine approach, computational output contributes to that work by locating overlooked associations and generating friction that redirects inquiry toward the source material. The graph record is therefore an ingredient in thick description, and sometimes a prompt for it, rather than its automated substitute.

To examine the ontological redistribution addressed by RQ1, both corpora were indexed under two experimental conditions: first using LightRAG's unmodified default schema, and second using the symmetrical `ant_ontology.yml` profile. Both conditions used the same instruction-rich JSON extraction mode (`ENTITY_EXTRACTION_USE_JSON=true`, without a populated few-shot example in the active JSON output format), the same chunking conditions, the same extraction model, and the same storage configuration, with the extraction profile serving as the intentionally varied component. The pilot consists of one run per condition and does not estimate run-to-run variance or isolate the independent effects of entity vocabulary, behavioral guidance, and template structure. The reported differences should therefore be read as diagnostic results from two configured pipeline runs rather than isolated causal effects of the profile.

Generalized symmetry is a principle of analytical treatment, not a quota requiring non-human nodes to outnumber human ones. Tables 2A and 2B below therefore compare what the two extraction profiles classify and make central rather than measuring symmetry directly. LightRAG normalizes stored `entity_type` strings to lowercase in Neo4j; the profile names are shown in PascalCase when referring to the configured categories. For the calculations, the human/collective category combines `person` and `organization` in the default condition and `humanactant` and `punctualizedcollective` in the symmetrical condition. Degree is the total number of relationships incident on a node. The residual total combines the prompt-defined `other` category with implementation-emergent `unknown` vertices. Because some `unknown` vertices represent untyped aliases of human or collective actors (such as the degree-11 SPAT variant in Table 5), the rightmost column of Table 2A describes stored type labels rather than a pure count of non-human actants.

### 4.2 The Adventure of Silver Blaze

To evaluate extraction symmetry on an open, bounded narrative before scaling to noisy organizational archives, the first test examines Arthur Conan Doyle's short story _The Adventure of Silver Blaze_ [[@doyleAdventureSilverBlaze1894]]. While classic detective fiction differs from real-world fieldwork because its clues and narrative closure are deliberately authored, scholarship in history, semiotics, and STS has noted that forensic detection exemplifies abductive inquiry (cf. [[@ginzburgMorelliFreudSherlock1980]]; [[@ecoSignThreeDupin1988]]; [[@latourAramisLoveTechnology1996]]). The forensic investigator reconstructs an unseen trial of strength by tracing material clues that resisted or modified human intentions. In Doyle's narrative, the disappearance of the racehorse Silver Blaze from King's Pyland stables and the death of his trainer, John Straker, cannot be reconstructed through human testimony alone; the plot hinges on non-human mediators, including a delicate surgical cataract knife, a dish of curried mutton masking powdered opium, and the famous watchdog that did nothing in the night-time.

The extracted records in Table 3 show that concrete material predicates are often preserved alongside instructive classification limits. In Node A and Edge 1, the surgical blade found in John Straker's hand is not reduced to a passive stage prop. Its stored node description records its physical state (clotted with blood), its intended sabotage function (nicking the racehorse's tendons at the King's Pyland stables), and its reversal during the struggle, while Edge 1's stored description and keywords (`accidental cut, self-injury`) register a material action in which the blade cuts Straker's own thigh. Node B and the descriptions attached to Edges 2 and 3 capture how `Curried Mutton` conceals `Powdered Opium` (`addition, contamination`) and subsequently becomes an evidentiary clue observed by Holmes (`clue, observation`), preserving empirical specificity without forcing ANT jargon onto the prose.

At the same time, Table 3 exposes two diagnostic frictions. First, `Knife`, `Curried Mutton`, `Powdered Opium`, and `Dog` were all assigned to the residual `other` category. The classification of curried mutton, powdered opium, dog, and the foam debris variants exposes a broader boundary in the tested profile. Biological organisms, chemical or prepared substances, and raw physical materials lacked dedicated substantive categories and therefore defaulted to `Other`; the profile’s `QuantifiedCommitment` category was separately limited to organizational allocations and commitments rather than physical measurements. For the surgical knife, cross-checking Table 3 against Table 5 shows that the generic and commercially specified references remained separate vertices: the full commercial identifier (`Ivory-Handled Knife (Weiss & Co., London)`) was extracted as a distinct `technicalartifact` vertex, while the shorter identifiers `Knife`, `Straker's Knife`, and `Small Knife` remained separate vertices classified as `other`. The graph therefore exhibits both naming fragmentation and type variation across differently specified references to the same material instrument, rather than type consolidation between merged forms.

Second, tracing the watchdog across the graph shows that the non-event was captured locally but fragmented across passages and identifiers. In the elliptical exchange in `chunk-012`, Edge 4 (`Dog` — `Sherlock Holmes`) records only that Holmes referred to the dog as a `point of curiosity`; it does not state that the dog’s failure to bark was the clue. In `chunk-015`, where Holmes explains the deduction explicitly, the extractor records that `The Dog` remained silent because it recognized the `Midnight Visitor`. It also records that the barking did not wake the two lads in the loft. The graph does not assemble these records into one abductive chain. Exact-string consolidation leaves `Dog`, `The Dog`, and `Dog (Hound)` as separate vertices. The generic `Midnight Visitor` remains unlinked to `John Straker`, whose role is disclosed in the following chunk, while another relationship attributes the observation to `Colonel` rather than Holmes. The difficulty is therefore not an absolute inability to represent non-action. The pipeline captures the silence where the source explains it explicitly, but does not necessarily connect elliptical dialogue, later explanation, speaker attribution, and cross-chunk identity.

### 4.3 Columbia STS-107

To test the apparatus on authentic organizational communications, the second test examines NASA's publicly released Kennedy Space Center (KSC) email collection [[@nasaKennedyEmployeeEMails2003]] from the January 2003 Space Shuttle Columbia (STS-107) mission. Retrieved via the Internet Archive Wayback Machine (September 26, 2012 snapshot), this corpus comprises 32 FOIA-released document files covering post-launch pad and film-screening reviews, Solid Rocket Booster Postflight Assessment Team (SPAT) reports, external tank foam build-record audits, technical briefings, and landing preparation. It therefore represents a selective Kennedy Space Center documentary collection rather than a complete archive of Debris Assessment Team or Mission Management Team (MMT) deliberations. In particular, the ingested KSC files do not document the Johnson Space Center and Langley correspondence regarding Department of Defense (DoD) satellite imagery requests that features prominently in later organizational analyses of the accident.

The collection nevertheless provides a heterogeneous organizational testbed for examining how physical observations and technical uncertainty are translated across media (cf. [[@latourAramisLoveTechnology1996]]; [[@tufteCognitiveStylePowerPoint2004]]; [[@vaughanSystemEffectsSlippages2005]]). Within these files, launch-film observations, foam-debris estimates, historical impact-test data, Crater calculations, briefing documents, email messages, and distribution lists participate differently in making an eventually fatal ascent debris strike assessable. Prior critiques of how technical briefings and summary statements derived from the Crater algorithm compressed engineering uncertainty [[@tufteCognitiveStylePowerPoint2004]] provide an interpretive context for the calculation and briefing inscriptions in the KSC collection. By contrast, sociological analyses of hierarchical routing and the imagery-request controversy [[@vaughanSystemEffectsSlippages2005]] concern a broader multi-center record that is not represented in the KSC email collection, serving here as a reminder of corpus partiality rather than as a relation the graph is expected to reproduce. The empirical question for this pilot is therefore specific: compared to the default baseline, how does `ant_ontology.yml` represent the documentary and calculative chain connecting historical test data, prediction models, damage-assessment briefings, contractor tasking, and email circulation within the boundaries of the KSC archive?

Broader retrospective accounts describe Ham’s role within the Mission Management Team, while sociological analyses examine the contested organizational pathways surrounding requests for external imagery [[@vaughanSystemEffectsSlippages2005]]. The ingested Kennedy Space Center correspondence provides a substantially narrower empirical trace. The node has two contributing `source_id` references and retains two uncondensed descriptions separated by `<SEP>`, both stating only that Ham appears in an email distribution or recipient list. Its single incident relationship likewise records only that `Email Message` includes Linda Ham in its recipient list. Neither record documents any action by Ham beyond her inclusion as a recipient. The Kennedy Space Center FOIA subset does not document the deliberations concerning Department of Defense imagery requests, but a researcher familiar with the wider Columbia controversy might read more into this edge than the KSC files support. The name is present in the archive, the extractor creates a node, and Neo4j contains a valid relationship. None of that documents managerial action. Description before characterization must therefore constrain the researcher as well as the model.

### 4.4 What Extraction and Consolidation Failures Reveal

The Neo4j databases were most informative where consolidation failed. First, because node merging at graph commitment relies on exact string matching on `entity_id`, surface-form aliases that the extraction model does not normalize during chunk reading remain split across disconnected vertices. Second, as described in Section 3.1, repeated node and relationship records accumulate `<SEP>`-delimited descriptions until LightRAG's configurable merge condition invokes an internal summarizer not governed by `ant_ontology.yml`, which can compress episodic actions on high-frequency hubs into an atemporal profile. Moreover, a node's incident degree in Neo4j (the number of distinct connected edges) differs from its accumulated chunk-description count (`<SEP>` segments): `Crater Equations` has an incident degree of 9 in Table 5 across three `<SEP>` chunk descriptions in Table 4, while `Linda Ham` has two `<SEP>` node descriptions from two chunks alongside a single deduplicated incident edge.

Third, endpoint canonicalization introduces a structural form of relational flattening. Although the extraction prompt distinguishes `source` and `target` fields, LightRAG consolidates inverse endpoint pairs under a single lexicographically ordered key, and Neo4j stores that consolidated relationship with a physical arrow whose orientation reflects string ordering rather than passage-level agency (as seen in Table 3, where `Curried Mutton` precedes `Sherlock Holmes`, and Table 4, where `Crater Equations` precedes `Review of Test Data`). The association remains navigable and its stored descriptions retain predicate detail, yet who observes, translates, delegates, or constrains whom cannot be inferred from topological arrow direction. This transformation makes the boundary between the Thick Machine and human thick description concrete: because an empirically directed action is indexed under an undirected consolidation key, reconstructing the direction of translation requires reading the stored descriptions and returning through `source_id` pointers to the contributing passages.

Case sensitivity (e.g. `SPAT` / `Spat`) and honorific or surname aliases (e.g. `Sherlock Holmes` and `Mr. Holmes`) represent largely mechanical duplications that dilute topological centrality. Deciding whether to merge them therefore requires returning to the contributing passages rather than applying a single string-similarity rule.

### 4.5 Implications for Qualitative Organizational Research

The pilot suggests that computational graphs can support organizational analysis in three limited but useful ways. First, they can redistribute attention toward mundane organizational participants, including models, spreadsheets, rules, filenames, recipient lists, and technical briefings. Second, they can make inconsistencies in naming and documentary circulation visible across a corpus. Third, they can serve as indexes through which researchers move between recurring entities and the passages in which those entities act.

These affordances do not automate organizational interpretation. A recipient edge does not establish participation, a high-degree node is not automatically an obligatory passage point, and multiple similar nodes do not necessarily represent an extraction error. The sociological task remains to establish what a relation did in a particular episode and whether apparent duplication reflects mechanical variation or multiple enactments of the object.

Extraction success depends on how the source inscribes the relation. The same non-event is weakly represented when conveyed through elliptical dialogue and more fully represented when restated as an explicit causal explanation. Even then, exact-string naming and chunk-local identity resolution may prevent the graph from integrating the successful extraction into the wider narrative chain.

The method is therefore most appropriate when the research question concerns how heterogeneous documents, calculations, rules, people, and technical systems become associated across a large archive. It is less suitable as a standalone method for interpreting elliptical dialogue, indirectly expressed absences, irony, tacit knowledge, cross-chunk identity, or relationships communicated primarily through visual layout.

## 5. Limitations and Next Steps for Fieldwork Archives

The next stage of this apparatus is to apply the profile to the pre-award RESILAKE archive. That archive includes Teams chats, meeting transcripts, email chains, proposal versions, slide decks, and multi-tab budget workbooks collected over several months. Three problems are especially immediate: preserving actor identity during pseudonymization, representing changes over time, and retaining visual, tabular, and formulaic structure during ingestion.

### 5.1 Role-Preserving Pseudonymization and Deictic References

The first practical bottleneck lies at the intersection of data-protection safeguards and LightRAG's name-based node consolidation. Because LightRAG merges nodes across chunks by matching `entity_id` strings, conventional de-identification methods introduce two failure modes. First, replacing personal or organizational names with generic redaction markers (`[REDACTED]`, `*****`) or unindexed category tags (`[PERSON]`, `<ORG>`) collapses distinct actors into a single super-node (`"PERSON"`), destroying multi-hop paths. Substituting random codes (`User_982`, `Org_14`) keeps nodes separate, yet it removes the functional and institutional cues ("Coordinator", "SME Partner", "Energy Sector") that the model uses to interpret relationships. Second, actors appear under shifting surface forms across emails, chats, and transcripts ("Dr. Kay Weber", "K. Weber", "Weber", "Kay"). Exact-string matching splits these variants into separate nodes or risks merging different people who share a surname. Chunking compounds this drift by detaching indexical references (such as _"our coordinator"_, _"that table"_, _"the budget"_, or _"the deadline"_) from their antecedents, creating redundant or fragmented nodes.

Addressing these issues prior to full-archive ingestion suggests separating named-entity pseudonymization from cross-chunk deictic resolution. Fully automated, one-pass redaction tools might suffer from high false-positive rates and lack cross-document consistency, but preliminary testing for this project has highlighted GDPR Pseudonymizer [[@deveauxGDPRPseudonymizerAIassisted2026]] as a promising human-in-the-loop foundation. Originally built for European compliance workflows in French, it combines regular-expression patterns, dictionary lookups, and neural Named Entity Recognition (NER) with an encrypted local mapping database that preserves consistent pseudonyms across multi-file batches. In correspondence with the program's author, initial exploratory tests adapting its NLP layer with a spaCy English transformer model (`en_core_web_trf`) have shown promising capability for consortium-level English. Within such a human-validated workflow, surface variants of a real name can be normalized onto a stable atomic pseudonym accompanied by separately controlled institutional or documentary metadata, preserving contextual cues for the analyst without baking permanent roles into `entity_id` strings. Cross-chunk deictic resolution will also require retaining document-level context such as sender, thread title, timestamp, version, and attachment names. Repeating complete recipient lists as ordinary extractable text in every chunk could reproduce the header-induced hub effects observed in the Columbia graph. Future ingestion should therefore store such information as structured metadata or instruct the extractor to use repeated headers for reference resolution while extracting circulation relations no more than once per document.

### 5.2 Temporal State Tracking

A second bottleneck concerns how vanilla LightRAG handles time across multi-month project negotiations. LightRAG accumulates descriptions without representing when each relation was valid. A partner may contest an allocation in November and accept a revised figure in January, yet both statements may appear in one merged profile. This makes it difficult to distinguish an active controversy from a later settlement. Versioned inscription nodes and dated episode nodes can preserve some sequential distinctions, but recurring relations between persistent actors still consolidate under the same canonically ordered endpoint pair and may lose temporal order during relationship-description merging.

To preserve the temporal dynamics of translation, future iterations can draw inspiration from temporal graph architectures such as Graphiti [[@rasmussenZepTemporalKnowledge2025]]. Within the unmodified LightRAG data model, dates or version identifiers could be embedded in relationship descriptions or keywords. Also raising the description-merge threshold could delay their condensation, although it would not provide formal temporal graph semantics and could increase description length. Longitudinal fieldwork across sequential drafts will also require an upstream version-aware ingestion procedure that distinguishes unchanged text from revised passages and preserves tracked changes and comments where available. Formal bi-temporal graph properties (`valid_at` and `invalid_at`) lie outside the present configuration-only approach and would require post-ingestion enrichment or a targeted extension to the storage layer.

### 5.3 Multimodal Ingestion

A third limitation follows from the pilot's text-only ingestion workflow. Pre-processing the Columbia PDFs with Adobe Acrobat's _Scan & OCR_ recovered text strings from briefing slides, tables, and plotted charts, but the resulting text-only representation might not preserve or reconstruct visual spatial hierarchy. In multi-partner RDI preparation, consequential translations occur inside structured documents and calculation devices, including Gantt charts, system diagrams, and multi-tab Excel workbooks where embedded formulas recalculate partner allocations across sheets. Flattening spreadsheets or slide decks into linear text erases the cell dependencies and visual relations through which these inscriptions constrain negotiation.

Scaling the pipeline to the RESILAKE corpus therefore requires evaluating layout-aware and multimodal ingestion alongside the present text-only workflow. Such processing capabilities [[@guoRAGAnythingAllinOneRAG2025]] have been integrated into LightRAG since v1.5.0. Layout-aware or vision-language parsing addresses visual and tabular presentation in slides and PDFs, though it introduces new model-dependent interpretations and does not expose spreadsheet formulas. Workbook ingestion should therefore inspect the OpenXML layer separately, retaining both displayed values (`QuantifiedCommitment`) and raw formulas or cross-sheet references (`TechnicalArtifact`) so that calculated outputs and their formulaic mediators remain distinguishable.

## 6. Conclusion

Moving from co-word analysis to LLM-generated graphs does not remove the problem of translation. It reproduces that problem inside parsing, chunking, prompting, naming, consolidation, and graph storage. The pilot shows that an externalized LightRAG profile can direct attention toward mundane documents, calculation tools, rules, and material constraints that generic classifications may treat too broadly.

The profile does not resolve the network. The watchdog clue is captured in an explicit later explanation but fragmented across chunks, node names, and unresolved identities; aliases split central actors, documentary self-references become hubs, and quantified debris estimates leak into entity identifiers. These failures are useful because they indicate where the graph must be checked against its source passages.

In relation to RQ1, the profile changed the differentiation and centrality of non-human traces most substantially in the Columbia archive, while leaving the human-centred topology of _Silver Blaze_ largely intact. In relation to RQ2, description before characterization preserved several concrete material and calculative relations but remained dependent on how explicitly the source inscribed them. In relation to RQ3, exact-string consolidation split mechanical aliases as well as potentially meaningful object variants, while description merging risked converting episodic actions into atemporal profiles.

The Linda Ham record condensed a methodological lesson: a valid graph relation may document circulation without documenting organizational action. This Thick Machine produced bounded, passage-grounded graph records. The frictions in the apparatus made the limitations and transformations of those records analytically visible. But thick description remains the researcher’s craft: reconstructing across documents and episodes how sociomaterial traces come to matter in situated practice.
